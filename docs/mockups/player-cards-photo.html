1 · Design (profile card)

The layout for all player cards. Every other kind builds on it and only changes the frame, accent colour and details. The KPIs are on the back, flip the card.

  1. TierBronze, Silver, Gold or Elite, small and right above the rating. Also sets the frame colour.
  2. Match RatingOverall value from 40 to 99 based on the player's role. The biggest number on the card.
  3. PositionWritten out subtly below the rating, separated by a thin line.
  4. Team logoTop right on a light background, so every logo stays readable.
  5. Action photoFills the whole card. A gradient towards the bottom keeps the text readable.
  6. Team, number, nameThe surname is the second most important element after the rating.
  7. Player type and titleThe archetype of the card with an icon, e.g. Playmaker.
  8. Match and rarityResult of the match and the rarity symbol. Clicking the card flips it and shows the KPIs and explanations.

2 · Performance specials

Same layout as the profile card, but darker, with fine glitter and its own accent colour. The surname runs along the right edge, the special label along the left. They are awarded by fixed rules based on the match data.

3 · Profile and base cards

Profile cards of the match (with rating and tier), followed by the base cards of the same players without any match data. For players without a freely licensed photo the card shows a placeholder in team colours.

4 · Card tiers

The tier follows from the player's Match Rating (MR) in his role. The higher the tier, the rarer the card and the more striking the frame. Special cards sit outside this scale because they are awarded for an event. Base cards have no tier.

5 · Player types and KPIs

Every player gets the type in which he was strongest in the match. The table shows which KPIs feed into each type and with what weight. The types are a proposal and can be adjusted freely.

1 · Percentile per KPIFor each metric we count how many of the 40 players were worse: 0 = weakest, 1 = best value in the match.
2 · Type scoreWeighted sum of the percentiles per the table: Σ weight × percentile. Percentages (pass %, faceoff %) only count from 10 passes or 5 faceoffs.
3 · Type and MRThe type with the highest score becomes the card type. MR = 40 + 59 × type score, i.e. 40 to 99.
4 · TierFrom the MR: Bronze < 80, Silver 80–89, Gold 90–96, Elite ≥ 97.
GoaliesOwn formula based on shots on goal: save % 45 %, high-danger saves (xG from 0.15) 35 %, goals saved above expected (GSAx) 20 %. Position GK, type “Goalie”.

        6 · Evolving Cards

        A card that grows with the tournament. It starts as a normal card after the first game. Each stage is unlocked by a clear event and brings a visible upgrade. Game 1 is real; the values for game 2 through the final are a fictional example.

          7 · Set bonus

          Sets give collecting a goal. Each set lists exactly which cards belong to it and comes with a reward. The progress shown is based on the cards in the collection above.

          8 · Collector, Legend and context cards

          Collector cards reward a completed set, legends honour earlier cup years (placeholder), the rest gives context. Moment, duo, rivals and coach are based on the real match data.

          Live dropWhen a goal is scored, every fan in the arena gets the moment card for a short time.
          Fan cardYour own photo in the arena as a card with date and seat, as a souvenir of the visit.

          9 · Pokémon-inspired editions

          Pokémon separates two things: how rare a card is (symbol at the bottom of the card) and how it is printed (reverse holo, full art, gold, illustration). We adopt that: every player card can additionally appear in a print edition, independent of tier and special.

          10 · Shadow Team

          Put your dream team on the ice with your own cards. Drag a card onto a position, or tap the card first and then the position. The team rating takes into account whether the position fits and how well the players work together.
